💡 Professor Viva Preparation & Architectural Q&A

Crisp answers to the 3 most frequent viva questions regarding compiler security and performance trade-offs.

1. Why not use Regex or Source-Level Linters?

Regex and AST linters have no concept of data-flow alias sets or control-flow dominance. They cannot tell whether a pointer dereference strictly executes before a null check, or whether a memset destination is truly dead at function exit across complex basic block graphs. Only LLVM's SSA IR and Clang's AST provide exact semantic precision without false alarms.

2. Why not just compile with -O0 or UBSan?

As shown in our benchmark measurements and the USENIX '23 paper, -O0 imposes +142% overhead (more than 2.4x slower) and UBSan imposes +180% to +220% overhead while still missing over 60% of real-world CISBs (such as dead-store wiping and CFG check reordering). CISB-Guard preserves aggressive -O3 speed everywhere, dropping overhead to < 1%.

3. Why is a Dual-Layer (AST + LLVM IR) design necessary?

ISpec1 Undefined Behavior bugs (like x + 100 < x) must be neutralized at the Clang AST level before the compiler front-end lowers them into IR under the No-UB model. Conversely, OSpec1 (Dead Store Elimination) and ISpec2 (Check Reordering) occur deep in middle-end LLVM IR optimization passes -- making a dual-layer defense structurally essential.
